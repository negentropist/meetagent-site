How do you know an AI agent is any good?

An evaluation framework for AI agents. Twelve dimensions, five levels each, written as behaviour you can observe. Every rating also says how strong the evidence is.

Evidence labels

Vendor claim
Stated in marketing, with no detail to check.
Documented
Described specifically in public technical, security or pricing documentation.
Tested
Measured with a published protocol on a fixed task set, independently of the vendor.
In production
Measured on real work over time, ideally against a comparison group.

Documented does not mean true, and tested does not mean it will work for you. A test supports a claim about those tasks, under those conditions.

Rules

  1. Rate for a stated task type. "Good at analysis" is not a rating. Performance on one task type doesn't transfer automatically to another.
  2. No overall score. Levels are ordered, but the gaps between them aren't equal, so averaging them would suggest a precision they don't have. Show the profile instead.
  3. Missing is not low. Use "not assessed" when there's no evidence, and "not applicable" when a dimension doesn't apply.
  4. Show version and date. Agents change quickly. Treat ratings older than 90 days as possibly out of date.
  5. Show the denominator. Write 13 of 18 runs, not 72%.
  6. Ratings can't be bought.
